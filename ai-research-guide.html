How to Use AI for Research

AI can help you organize research questions, summarize information you provide, and identify topics that need further investigation. However, an AI answer is not automatically correct or supported by reliable evidence.

1. Start with a Clear Research Question

A focused question usually produces a more useful result than a broad request. Instead of asking “Tell me about online business,” ask “What are three common challenges beginners face when starting a freelance writing service?”

Help me plan research on this question: [write your question]. Break it into smaller questions, identify important terms to define, suggest what types of reliable sources I should look for, and list assumptions I should avoid making. Do not invent facts or citations.

2. Use AI to Organize Your Work

Ask AI to create an outline, group your notes by theme, or explain difficult terms in simpler language. If you need an accurate summary, provide the original text and ask the tool to stay within it.

Organize the research notes below into clear themes. Separate facts, opinions, open questions, and conclusions. Do not add information that is not in my notes. If a point is unclear, label it as unclear. Notes: [paste your notes].

3. Check Sources Independently

When a tool gives you a factual claim, find the original source and read the relevant section yourself. Prefer official documentation, primary research, recognized institutions, and sources that explain their methods. Check the publication date when information may have changed.

  • Open the original page rather than trusting a quoted snippet.
  • Check whether the source actually supports the claim.
  • Compare important claims with another credible source.
  • Check author, date, evidence, and possible conflicts of interest.

4. Evaluate a Source

Help me evaluate this source for my research. Assess its author or organization, publication date, evidence, methods, possible bias, and relevance to my question. Clearly separate what can be established from the information provided and what still needs checking. Research question: [question]. Source details or text: [paste details].

5. Make a Research Summary

After checking your sources, create a summary that distinguishes confirmed information from uncertainty. Keep a record of the original URLs, titles, authors, and dates so you can return to the evidence later.

Using only the verified notes I provide, write a concise research summary. Include the main findings, evidence for each finding, limitations, and unanswered questions. Do not create references or claim that a source says something unless that is present in my notes. Notes: [paste verified notes and source details].

6. Protect Private Information

Do not paste passwords, identity numbers, private customer details, confidential business documents, or sensitive personal information into an AI tool unless you have confirmed that its policies and your permissions allow it.

Common Questions

Can AI be used as a research source?

AI can help with planning and understanding, but important claims should be supported by original, credible sources.

Can AI make up references?

Yes, AI tools can produce inaccurate or nonexistent references. Always open and verify every cited source.

Which sources should I trust?

Trust depends on the question. Official records and primary sources are often useful, but you should still examine the evidence and context.

Remember: Treat AI output as a starting point. Your conclusions should be based on evidence you have checked yourself.
